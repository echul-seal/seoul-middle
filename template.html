A question, taken literally

Where is the exact middle of Seoul?

"Middle" is not one thing. It's a choice of what you're averaging: land, people, trains, a river, or history. I computed thirteen answers from real data and wrote down how each one was reached.

    Why they disagree

    Shape answers cluster on Namsan. The area centroid, the pole of inaccessibility and the 2009 survey all sit within 600 m of each other on the mountain. Seoul is a rough blob with the Han cutting through, and the mountain happens to sit near where the land balances.

    Box answers drift west. The bounding box and the smallest enclosing circle ignore everything except a few extreme points of the outline. Move one tip of Gangseo or Dobong and they move; the rest of the city doesn't count. That's why they land by City Hall and Seoul Station, 1.5 to 2 km from the shape answers.

    People pull it south. More residents live south of the river (Gangnam, Songpa, Gwanak) than the shape alone suggests, so the population centers slide down Namsan's southern face toward Itaewon. By day, when offices in Gangnam and Yeouido fill up, it slides further south still.

    Time pulls it north-east, into the old downtown. The subway was built radially out of the four-gate city. The stations where lines converge, Sindang and Dongdaemun History & Culture Park, are where every other station is closest by train. Geometry doesn't know this. Riders do.

    The markers are answers to older questions. The road origin at Gwanghwamun is where distances to other cities are measured from: a center for the road network, not for the land. The 1896 stone in Insa-dong marked the middle of the walled city, which is a fraction of today's Seoul.
